Picture a small harbour town in the far north, just after dawn. Fishing boats knock against the jetty, their ropes creaking, while a few early joggers puff along the shore. Why would anyone choose to live here? Ask the locals, and they'll shrug: the views, the quiet, the sheer pleasure of the sea air. But each winter the gales howl back, and for one long, noisy month, that stubborn village measures its worth in the shutters, roof tiles and old garden paths the huge storms took.
